It is forecasted that in 2028, over six billion people worldwide will use social media, with 
Facebook being the most popular, accounting for roughly 2.9 billion users (Dixon, 2024). When 
MySpace and Friendster first launched in 2003, the initial purpose of social media was to maintain 
contact with family and friends, but this model soon changed to sharing, learning and marketing. Media 
corporations could imprint their ostensible ideologies on new demographics, which were previously 
difficult to acquire. Bring forth the rise of social media influencers. The collaboration between brands 
and influencers has innovatively altered how marketing is administered, but it has called into question 
their level of integrity. Social Media influencers' (SMIs) trustworthiness is intricately connected with 
their capacity to maintain authenticity, their portrayal of narratives to establish trust, and the 
juxtaposition of regulatory frameworks governing traditional versus digital media.  
 
Firstly, SMIs’ omnipresence has dominated the media landscape, leveraging vast followings 
with affiliated cohorts to deliver dynamic endorsements via personalised and relatable content. SMIs’ 
authenticity refers to their trustworthiness and message truthfulness. Wellman et al. (2020) determined 
that ethical frameworks are necessary for brands approaching influencers to produce sponsor content. 
Creators must exercise discretion on collaborations, as partnerships may not complement their brand 
identity; consequently, lucrative offers are rejected to maintain trust and credibility. Without a formal 
ethical code, authenticity serves as the moral compass for SMIs, highlighting the need for formal 
guidelines to ensure honesty and professionalism in the sector (Wellman et al., 2020). While authenticity 
may seem straightforward initially, SMIs face increasing challenges as their popularity and 
endorsements grow, presenting dilemmas in maintaining authenticity amid commercial pressures and 
public expectations. Balaban and Szambolics (2021) state that influencers differentiate brands, 
promoting a sense of self-perceived authenticity. The research emphasises the need for genuine brand 
collaboration, ensuring sponsored content aligns with influencers’ value and creativity, benefiting both 
parties.  
 
Secondly, the level of trust dictates SMIs and media credibility, prompting consumers to change 
their mindset from the urge to buy to actual impulsive purchases on social networking sites. Reliable 
and transparent information is vital for establishing a good rapport in influence marketing. Shamim and 
Islam (2022) suggested that storytelling content, in which SMIs share personal experiences, can 
significantly strengthen consumer trust. More often enough, disclaimers usually state if any 
premeditated sponsorship deals are in place, enhancing the audience’s trust in their recommendations. 
The research also highlights that storytelling elements and shared personal experiences bolster trust, 
resulting from the consumer's urge to buy to impulsive purchasing (Shamim & Islam, 2022). Although

perceived interactivity, like responding to comments and engaging in discussions, does not directly 
generate sales, it helps influencers foster better relationships with audiences. Influencers who establish 
themselves as professionals in niche markets by sharing valuable insights appear more trustworthy, 
indicating that informational value is core to building trust. While transparency plays a crucial role, it 
does not necessarily equate to trust; it merely provides the foundation for building it.  
 
Finally, with the rapid evolution of information, conventional media such as television, radio, 
and the printed press may struggle to maintain relevance and credibility in the digital age. Weismueller 
et al. (2020) compare traditional media and SMIs, emphasising regulation practices and perception 
towards consumer purchase intentions. Since traditional advertising is heavily regulated with clear 
stipulations, celebrities are specifically chosen due to their attractiveness and broad appeal. On the 
contrary, SMIs are seen as more relatable and engage directly with niche audiences but could be 
observed as more manipulative. Traditional media is perceived as a transparent disclosure of genuine 
endorsements, but this homogeneity may hinder its impact on consumer purchasing intentions. 
Furthermore, both traditional and new media can be used effectively when aligned, as the former 
increases broad awareness, and the latter influences consumer preferences (de Vries, 2017).  
 
In conclusion, the exponential rise in social media has accentuated the transformative impact 
of communication and marketing, which has, on the first conception, completely evolved from 
connecting friends and family to giving rise to SMIs with brand collaborations. Being authentic allows 
influencers to create trustworthy endorsements but becomes challenging as popularity grows. 
Credibility is determined by transparency levels, which alone does not guarantee trust. Traditional 
media must reevaluate their practices and collaborate with SMIs; otherwise, they may become obsolete 
as digital platforms dominate. More regulation may ensure better honesty and integrity endorsements, 
which could maintain the influencer’s credibility in the long term.
