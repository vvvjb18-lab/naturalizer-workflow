Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) occupy a complex 
position between tightly regulated clinical therapy., and the broader consumer-driven 
weight-loss market. In 2021, the number of adult males and females living with 
overweight or obesity was estimated at 1.00 billion and 1.11 billion people, respectively 
(GBD 2021 Adult BMI Collaborators, 2025). These pharmacological products were 
originally intended to replicate hormones involved in the regulating blood sugar, 
suppressing appetite and delaying gastrointestinal emptying, and were, used for specific 
clinical purposes. However, driven by their profound weight loss effect, they have now 
gone beyond the medical category and become widely consumed beauty industry 
products (Ferhatbegović et al., 2023). This change has brought about controversy 
regarding healthcare policy, clinical practice, and public perception. The critical 
analysis of GLP-1 RAs thus needs to focus on the clinical and psychosocial advantages 
of medications and on the metabolic and the psychological risk. 
From a clinical perspective., the GLP-1 receptor agonists seem to have more 
advantages than weight loss alone. According to Garvey et al. (2022), the patients 
undergoing these treatments had a mean weight loss of 15.2% in two years. These 
adiposity reductions can enhance metabolic control and, according to Jensterle et al. 
(2024), are linked to a reduced rate of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), 
such as myocardial infarction and stroke. In this perspective, the drug is not only a 
weight-management tool, but a obesity-related morbidity and mortality prevention 
measure, particularly for individuals with elevated cardiometabolic risk. These benefits 
may also help reduce future pressure on the health care system, although this broader 
impact requires further investigation (Jensterle et al., 2024). 
 


In addition to clinical measures, weight loss that can be achieved through such 
drugs has psychosocial benefit. Patients can have less weight stigmatization, feel more 
confident in social places, and no longer feel the psychological burder of numerous 
failed attempts to lose weight. Nevertheless, as Jensen et al. (2025) note, these benefits 
depend on long-term weight loss, and the degree of positive results will depend on the 
patient's psychological resilience and social support. This suggest that although the 
psychosocial value of these drugs may be important., it may vary based on individual 
and situational circumstances. 
One of the issues, though, is whether the weight loss will be sustained over time 
in terms of metabolism. Prokopidis et al. (2025) report that there is a concomitant loss 
of muscle mass when these agents are used and a great part of the weight lost is 
recovered when the treatment is stopped. This trend suggests that long-term results can 
be reliant on subsequent use. This is further supported by Moscucci et al. (2026), who 
note that 25-40% of total weight loss from GLP-1 receptor agonists is explained by 
muscle. mass loss. This loss may decrease.long-term mobility and increase the 
likelihood of the weakness, instability and incapacity. The combination of these 
metabolic problems implies that pharmacological weight loss cannot be adequate. 
permanent remedy unless it is supplemented by other. nutritional lifestyle interventions. 
From the perspective of psychiatry, these shortcomings are further aggravated. The 
risk not only comes directly from the physiological effects of severe appetite 
suppression, but also from the psychological burden and distorted social norms related 
to long-term dependence on drugs to control weight. The aesthetic requirements of 
society for the ideal body type, coupled with the pressure to maintain the weight loss 
effect of drugs, may further increase the psychological burden of patients. Consequently, 
some patients,.especially those with pre-existing psychological weaknesses, may 


experience body shape anxiety, depressive symptoms, or restrictive eating behaviors 
(Krug et al., 2025). In such cases., mental health may be sacrificed in pursuit of the slim 
figure achieved through medication. 
Overall, the GLP-1 RAs are subject to trade-offs.,They may offer .genuine clinical 
and psychosocial benefits, yet there may be risks of physical dependence and 
psychological. risk. It is against such risks that they may not be viewed as readily 
available cosmetic weight-loss products, but should be viewed instead as prescriptive 
drugs., depending on their specific situations. In long term, it is only under medical 
control along with social education based on body diversity and inclusiveness that the 
dependency on radical means of weight control in dealing with obesity can be mitigated.
